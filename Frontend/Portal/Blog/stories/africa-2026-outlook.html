Over the past few months, I’ve been working on a structural outlook for Africa as it enters the mid-2020s. Not a prediction piece. Not a manifesto. A briefing.

The premise is simple:

Africa’s risk over the next decade is not scarcity. It is misalignment. Economic models designed for extraction are being applied in an intelligence-driven global economy. Institutions built for linear environments are being asked to operate in exponential systems. Education pathways optimized for credentials are feeding a labor market organized around capabilities.

The result is a widening gap between participation in the global economy and performance within it.

This outlook examines five structural shifts already underway: This outlook examines five structural shifts already underway:

  • The reallocation of value from extraction to intelligence
  • The growing cost of institutional friction
  • Financial fragmentation and currency volatility
  • Labor market compression and capability misalignment
  • Governance as a function of execution speed

None of these shifts are speculative. All are observable. All are already shaping outcomes.

The document is written for board members, senior executives, policymakers, and institutional leaders responsible for long-horizon decisions. Entrepreneurs and operators will find implications throughout, but the analysis is intentionally system-level.
